Electronics & Embedded Systems

The robot's nervous system: power, circuit boards, motor drivers and firmware

About / Technical groups / Electronics & Embedded Systems

The Electronics & Embedded Systems group gives the robot power and connects every part of it. Members design the battery and power system, build circuit boards and wiring, drive the motors and write the firmware on the microcontrollers that read sensors and send commands.

Most robot failures at competitions are electrical: a loose connector, a brown-out or a noisy signal. This group designs for reliability and safety, with fuses, emergency stops, clear wiring standards and tests before every run.

What the group works on

Power systems

Battery choice, power distribution, fuses, voltage regulators and the emergency-stop circuit.

PCB design

Schematics, layout and assembly of control, driver and interface boards.

Motor control

Motor drivers, encoders and current and speed loops for wheels, arms and leg joints.

Embedded firmware

Real-time code on STM32 and ESP32 microcontrollers: drivers, timing and fault handling.

Communication

CAN, UART, SPI and I2C between boards, and the link to the onboard computer.

Wiring and testing

Harnesses, connectors, labelling, noise reduction and test procedures before every run.

Engineering foundations

A few of the equations members use in this group. Training sessions build up to them step by step.

Battery runtime

\[t \approx \frac{C\,V_{\text{nom}}\,\eta}{P_{\text{avg}}}\]

Estimated operating time from battery capacity C, nominal voltage, system efficiency and average power draw.

DC motor model

\[\tau = K_t\,I, \quad V = I\,R + K_e\,\omega\]

Torque is proportional to current; the supply voltage covers the resistive drop and the back-EMF at speed ω.

PWM average voltage

\[V_{\text{avg}} = D\,V_{\text{bus}}\]

A motor driver sets the effective voltage through the duty cycle D of its switching signal.

Conductor loss

\[P_{\text{loss}} = I^2 R\]

Heat in wires and connectors. Used to size cables and choose connectors for high-current motor lines.

Discrete PI current loop

\[u_k = K_p\,e_k + K_i\,T_s \sum_{j=0}^{k} e_j\]

The inner control loop that runs on the microcontroller at a fixed sample time T_s.

RC low-pass filter

\[f_c = \frac{1}{2\pi R C}\]

Cut-off frequency of a simple filter that removes switching noise from sensor signals.

What we expect from members

Expectations grow with the member tier. Tiers are reviewed regularly, so members can move up as their skills grow.

Tier 3 · General

Curiosity about circuits and microcontrollers. Learns soldering and lab safety in introductory sessions.

Tier 2 · Training

Can read schematics, solder reliably, program a microcontroller and use a multimeter and oscilloscope; follows battery safety rules.

Tier 1 · Advanced

Owns the electrical design of a robot, designs power and control boards, sets wiring standards and mentors Tier 2 members.

Before you join: Basic circuit theory, a microcontroller project (Arduino, STM32 or ESP32) and some C programming are good starting points. No member is expected to know everything at the start.
