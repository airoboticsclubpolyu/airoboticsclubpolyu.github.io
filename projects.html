Projects

Drones, robot dogs, unmanned vehicles and competition robots, built by students with faculty and TAS Lab mentors.

Current Projects

Seed projects are subject to Faculty approval and are reviewed at mid-term. Members join through their technical group.

Competition · 2026–27

Robocon 2027

Our main competition team for 2026–27. The team will enter the Robocon Hong Kong contest, the route to ABU Robocon 2027 in Solo, Indonesia, on the theme “The Pursuit of Mustika Nusantara”. Two robots per team must handle game objects, build towers and work together under match time.

Mechanisms · embedded control · autonomous navigation · vision · match strategy

Seed project · 2026–27

Drones for building inspection and façade cleaning

An autonomous UAV that flies close to building façades to inspect them and clean windows, following TAS Lab's work on cleaning drones for tall buildings in Hong Kong. Students work on positioning near glass and concrete, contact control and safe flight procedures.

Flight control · GNSS/visual positioning · aerial manipulation · safety

Seed project · 2026–27

Robotic guide dog

A quadruped robot that can guide visually impaired people along pavements and indoor routes: detecting obstacles, kerbs and stairs, following a planned path and giving clear feedback to the user.

Legged locomotion · perception · path planning · human–robot interaction

Seed project · 2026–27

Autonomous ground vehicle

A small unmanned vehicle for campus delivery and patrol. Students build the full autonomy stack, from LiDAR and camera perception and mapping to localisation, planning and vehicle control.

ROS · SLAM · LiDAR and camera fusion · motion planning

Preparation · for 2028

RoboCup China Open 2028

A long-term team preparing for the RoboCup China Open in 2028, starting with robot platform selection, skills training and small internal matches in 2026–27.

Multi-robot coordination · vision · locomotion · AI decision-making

How Projects Run

Every funded project and competition team follows the same four stages.

  1. ProposalPurpose, technical plan, team, budget and milestones, presented to the supervisors.
  2. Build & testRegular progress meetings with mentors; design reviews before major spending.
  3. Mid-term reviewProgress report, expenditure and demonstration decide whether support continues.
  4. HandoverCAD, code, test logs and a trained successor for the next cohort.
