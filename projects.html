Projects

Our Projects

All club work is organised as projects. Application projects solve a real-world problem; competition projects build robots for an external contest. Both follow the same rules below.

Drone project · 2026–27

Drones for building inspection and façade cleaning

An autonomous UAV that flies close to building façades to inspect them and clean windows, a real need for Hong Kong's high-rise city. Students work on positioning near glass and concrete, contact control and safe flight procedures.

Flight control · GNSS/visual positioning · aerial manipulation · safety

Robot dog project · 2026–27

Robotic guide dog

A quadruped robot that can guide visually impaired people along pavements and indoor routes: detecting obstacles, kerbs and stairs, following a planned path and giving clear feedback to the user.

Legged locomotion · perception · path planning · human–robot interaction

Unmanned vehicle project · 2026–27

Autonomous ground vehicle

A small unmanned vehicle for campus delivery and patrol. Students build the full autonomy stack, from LiDAR and camera perception and mapping to localisation, planning and vehicle control.

ROS · SLAM · LiDAR and camera fusion · motion planning

Competition project · 2026–27

Robocon 2027

The club's main competition project for 2026–27. The team will enter the Robocon Hong Kong contest, the route to ABU Robocon 2027 in Solo, Indonesia, on the theme “The Pursuit of Mustika Nusantara”. Two robots must handle game objects, build towers and work together under match time.

Mechanisms · embedded control · autonomous navigation · vision · match strategy

Competition project · for 2028

RoboCup China Open 2028

A long-term project preparing for the RoboCup China Open in 2028, starting with robot platform selection, skills training and small internal matches in 2026–27.

Multi-robot coordination · vision · locomotion · AI decision-making

How Projects Run

Every project, including competition projects, goes through the same six stages.

  1. Team formationTier 1 members form and lead a team. Tier 2 members join under supervision, drawn from different technical groups.
  2. Proposal & defenceA written proposal with an itemised budget and measurable targets, presented to a review panel with questions.
  3. ApprovalNo project is funded automatically. The panel may approve, ask for revisions, reduce the budget or reject.
  4. Build & testBi-weekly progress with mentors, design reviews before major purchases and fabrication support from Industrial Centre engineers.
  5. Mid-term reviewA progress and maintenance report plus a presentation. The result is pass, conditional pass or fail.
  6. Delivery & handoverFinal demonstration or competition, complete documentation and a trained successor for the next cohort.
What a proposal must answer
Why PolyU should support the project; what will be built and how; why this team can deliver; the target result, such as a specific competition award, and how success will be measured; why each budget item is needed; and the milestones, risks and safety controls.
Funding and purchasing
Every purchase needs a quotation and approval before buying. Teams first check club and University inventory and reuse existing equipment wherever possible, and keep the original receipt, purchase link and payment record for every item. Project funding is released in stages, typically 40% after approval, 40% after a passed mid-term review and 20% after the final demonstration and documentation.
Competition entries
Only Tier 1 members can form a team to enter a competition. When more than one team wants to represent PolyU, an internal competition decides who goes.
Accountability
Each team states its target result in the proposal. A team that misses its target without a convincing external reason will normally not be funded in the next cycle, and its Tier 1 members are reviewed. A failed mid-term review stops the project; the team returns club assets and hands over its documentation.
Workspace
Industrial Centre workspace is allocated to project teams by application for a set period. Use is checked; space used less than about 60% of the time goes back to the Industrial Centre.
Documentation
Every project keeps a repository with requirements, CAD, code, bill of materials, test logs and lessons learned. A project is closed only after a complete handover.
