No. Beginners are placed in Tier 3 and start with guided workshops and foundation tasks. Experience helps with placement, but curiosity, consistency and willingness to learn matter most.

PolyU students from any department who are interested in robotics and AI. Members work in one of four technical groups: mechanical design, electronics and embedded systems, perception and sensors, or software and AI.

Apply online through the QR code on the recruitment poster, take a short baseline written test, then attend a structured interview. Successful applicants are placed into a tier and a technical group, attend an induction, and complete safety training and sign the club rules before working in the Industrial Centre. See the Join page.

The club recruits once a year, in September. If you missed this year's round, you will need to wait for the next September intake. Follow the News page for the announcement.

Commitment and rules

At the interview you tell us how much time you can give, and that becomes your commitment. Tier 2 members join regular bi-weekly training; Tier 1 members carry project and competition work with deadlines. We expect every member to keep the commitment they made. Members who repeatedly fall short will be advised to leave the club.

Every member completes safety training and signs the club's safety, equipment and membership rules. These cover safe working, keeping the workspace clean and exits clear, returning tools and equipment, signing out, and using funds and equipment responsibly. You must follow the rules at all times.

A first violation brings a written warning and retraining, and access may be restricted. A second violation means removal from the club, including loss of workspace and team access. A single serious breach, such as deliberately unsafe conduct, dishonesty or misuse of funds or equipment, can lead to immediate removal.

Tiers and promotion

  • Tier 3, General Member: seminars, salons, company visits and introductory workshops. No external competition representation.
  • Tier 2, Training Member: structured bi-weekly training and supervised hands-on work; can form or join project and competition teams.
  • Tier 1, Advanced Member: leads project and competition teams, mentors junior members and represents PolyU when selected.

Every promotion needs a fresh assessment and interview and the approval of the supervisors and the mentor team; past membership does not give automatic promotion. To move from Tier 3 to Tier 2 you normally need at least 70% attendance, a passed foundation assessment in one technical area, real project participation and a recommendation. To move from Tier 2 to Tier 1 you need outstanding results: strong project outcomes, or a strong result in a competition. See the promotion rules.

Yes. Tiers are reviewed regularly and are not permanent. Low attendance, insufficient contribution, repeatedly missed commitments, abandoning a project, unsafe conduct or misuse of facilities can lead to demotion or removal after a documented review, in which you can respond. If you have academic difficulties, the club can reduce your workload or pause your participation.

Projects and competitions

In 2026–27: a UAV for building inspection and patrol, a robotic guide dog, an autonomous ground vehicle, the Robocon 2027 competition project and RoboCup 2028 preparation. See the Projects page.

Start with the group that interests you most: mechanical design, electronics and embedded systems, perception and sensors, or software and AI. Project teams combine members from several groups.

You must be at least a Tier 2 member: only Tier 1 and Tier 2 members can form a team to enter a competition or apply for a project. Tier 3 members can take part in open activities but cannot enter external competitions. Each team submits a written proposal with a target result and budget and defends it in a presentation. If more than one team wants to represent PolyU, an internal competition decides who goes. See How Projects Run.

The club is exploring credit routes for project and competition members, such as the Undergraduate Research and Innovation Scheme (URIS), free electives and capstone projects. Training and workshops are 0-credit. Details will be announced when confirmed.

The club's Director is Ir Professor MAN Hau-chung, Dean of the Faculty of Engineering, and the Chief Supervisor is Prof. Weisong Wen. Staff-level supervisors from AAE, EEE and ME, the Industrial Centre's Coach Advisor and a PhD-level mentor team support the technical work. See the Team page.
