Q&A

Questions students ask before and after joining

Joining

No. Beginners start in Tier 3 with guided workshops and foundation tasks. Experience helps with placement, but curiosity, consistency and willingness to learn matter most.

PolyU students from any department who are interested in robotics and AI. Members work across mechanical design, electronics and embedded systems, perception and sensors, and software and AI.

Apply through the QR code or form shared during recruitment, take a short baseline written test, then attend a structured interview. Successful applicants are placed into a tier and a technical group and complete a safety induction. See the Join page.

The main recruitment runs at the start of the academic year, around August and September. If you missed it, email robosig@connect.polyu.hk and ask about the next intake.

Time and tiers

  • Tier 3: no fixed hours. Join talks, workshops and introductory activities.
  • Moving to Tier 2: attend at least 70% of relevant training over one semester and contribute to a project.
  • Tier 1: competition and project work, usually about 10 hours a week, with at least 80% attendance to stay in the tier.
  • Tier 3, General Member: explore through talks, workshops and demonstrations.
  • Tier 2, Training Member: structured training, practical tasks and project contributions.
  • Tier 1, Advanced Member: competition teams and funded projects; leads modules and mentors others.

From Tier 3 to Tier 2 you need at least 70% attendance, a passed foundation skills assessment, a project contribution and a recommendation from a mentor. From Tier 2 to Tier 1 you need at least four of six criteria, including technical contribution, commitment, documentation and leadership, with supervisor approval. See the full rules.

Members have a mid-semester check-in and an end-of-semester review. Nobody is moved down without written notice, a mentor, an improvement plan and a four-week probation period, and you can appeal within one week. Approved leave protects your tier. Serious or repeated safety breaches can lead to removal.

Projects and competitions

In 2026–27: the Robocon 2027 team, a façade inspection and cleaning drone, a robotic guide dog, an autonomous ground vehicle and RoboCup 2028 preparation. See the Projects page.

Start with the group that interests you most: mechanical design, electronics and embedded systems, perception and sensors, or software and AI. You can move across groups later as you learn how full robots fit together.

Competition teams are drawn from Tier 1 and strong Tier 2 members. Selection is based on assessed skills, the quality of your project contributions, attendance and documentation, with supervisor approval.

Faculty supervisors from AAE, EEE and ME and the Industrial Centre give direction, and postdocs and PhD students from TAS Lab support hands-on technical work. See the Team page.
