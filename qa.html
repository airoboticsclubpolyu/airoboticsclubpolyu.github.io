Joining

Getting started with the club.

Everyone starts as a Level 3 Registered Participant, whatever their experience.

No. Beginners start with guided workshops and foundation tasks. Experience helps, but curiosity and consistency matter most.

PolyU students who are interested in robotics and AI. The club is a Faculty of Engineering community, and members work across mechanical design, electronics, perception and AI, software, and competition operations.

Submit your interest through the joining form shared during recruitment, attend an orientation session, then choose a technical path. The full steps are on the Join page.

Time & Levels

Commitment, membership levels, and reviews.

The criteria are public. Individual review results stay confidential.

It depends on your level:

  • Level 3: no fixed hours. Join talks, workshops, and introductory activities.
  • Moving to Level 2: attend at least 70% of workshops and training over one semester, and contribute to a project.
  • Moving to Level 1: one of the criteria is at least 10 hours per week for one semester.
  • Level 3, Registered Participant: explore through talks, workshops, and demonstrations.
  • Level 2, Developing Member: receive training, join internal projects, and build skills in at least one area.
  • Level 1, Advanced Core Member: lead modules, mentor teammates, prepare competitions, and represent the club.

From Level 3 to Level 2 you need workshop attendance, a passed foundation skills check, a project contribution, and an endorsement from a Level 1 member or PhD mentor. From Level 2 to Level 1 you need at least four of six criteria, including two Level 1 nominations with faculty approval. See the full membership rules.

Members get a mid-semester check-in and an end-of-semester review. Nobody is moved down without written notice, a mentor, an improvement plan, and a four-week probation period. You can appeal within one week, and re-promotion is possible after one semester. Approved leave protects your level.

Projects & Competitions

What you will build and who guides you.

Members start in one technical area and move across tracks as they learn how full robotics systems connect.

Current tracks are the robot dog platform, the self-driving logistics vehicle, vision, SLAM and edge AI, and embedded systems and reliability. See the Projects page for details.

Start with the area that interests you most: mechanical design, electronics and control, perception and AI, or software and simulation. You can move across tracks later.

Pick a module and complete its foundation tasks, own a subsystem deliverable, then take part in integrated tests. Teams are selected on contribution quality, documentation, attendance, and faculty-approved readiness. See the competition tracks.

Faculty supervisors from AAE, EEE, and ME give academic direction, and postdoctoral, PhD, and MPhil mentors from TAS Lab support hands-on technical work. See the Team page.

Yes. Experienced members can lead project modules, mentor teammates, prepare competitions, and improve technical documentation.
