PolyU FENG AI & Robotics Club

About

Our vision, how we work and who guides us

The PolyU FENG AI & Robotics Club is a student engineering community supported by the Faculty of Engineering (FENG) of The Hong Kong Polytechnic University. Members from across PolyU design, build and test real robots, from autonomous drones and robot dogs to unmanned ground vehicles and competition robots, and learn how mechanical design, electronics, perception and AI come together in one working system.

Our vision

To build a world-class robotics club, first in Hong Kong, then in Asia and the world, and to cultivate outstanding students who can take a real problem all the way to a working, reliable robot.

We measure ourselves by our students. A club member should be able to define a problem, design across mechanics, electronics, sensing and software, build and test under real deadlines, and explain the result honestly. Our motto, from the club's founding plan, is to make robots that contribute to society, and to become engineers who contribute to society.

Our mission

  • Identify and develop talented students through rigorous hands-on training, interdisciplinary teamwork and high-level competition.
  • Give every member close guidance from Faculty of Engineering staff, Industrial Centre engineers and PhD-level mentors.
  • Prepare the strongest members for research, including undergraduate research and future MPhil or PhD study, and for demanding careers in robotics, AI, autonomous vehicles, logistics automation and aerospace.
  • Build robotic technologies with educational, scientific and social value, and pass every design, line of code and lesson on to the next cohort.

International and industry exchange

A world-class club has to measure itself against the best teams outside PolyU.

  • International competition. The Robocon 2027 project enters the Hong Kong contest, the route to ABU Robocon 2027 in Solo, Indonesia, and a RoboCup China Open 2028 project has started. Through these events members meet and compete with university teams from across Asia.
  • Learning from leading teams. Members study how top teams in Robocon and DJI RoboMaster, such as CUHK, HKUST, HKU, Tsinghua University and Shanghai Jiao Tong University, organise training, engineering and knowledge transfer.
  • Industry partners. Companies in legged robots, embodied AI, autonomous logistics and intelligent transport give members access to real robot platforms, problems from industry, mentoring, company visits and internship routes.
  • Visits and outreach. Company visits, talks and public showcases, such as the PolyU Student Orientation, connect members with engineers and the wider community.

Leadership and supervision

The club's Director is Ir Professor MAN Hau-chung (文効忠), Dean of the Faculty of Engineering, Cheng Yick-chi Chair Professor in Manufacturing Engineering and Chair Professor of Materials Engineering. Professor Man provides the club's strategic direction and Faculty-level support for its long-term development.

Prof. Weisong Wen of the Department of Aeronautical and Aviation Engineering is the Chief Supervisor and coordinates the club's programme. Staff-level supervisors from AAE, EEE and ME advise on technical direction, and LEE Kam-pui Jonathan, Engineering Manager at the PolyU Industrial Centre, is the club's Coach Advisor for hands-on engineering, fabrication and workspace use. A PhD-level mentor team works with students week to week. See the Team page.

Four technical groups

Every member belongs to one technical group. Project teams draw members from several groups, so each robot is built by people with complementary skills.

Mechanical Design

CAD, structures and mechanisms, materials, fabrication, assembly and maintainability. Turns ideas into working robot bodies.

Electronics & Embedded Systems

Power, wiring and PCBs, motor control, embedded computing and communications. Makes every subsystem run reliably together.

Perception & Sensors

Cameras, LiDAR, GNSS and IMU, calibration, state estimation, sensor fusion and mapping. Lets robots understand where they are.

Software & AI

Software architecture, planning and control, simulation, computer vision and machine learning. Gives robots their autonomy.

Three member tiers

Every member joins through the same application, written test and interview and is placed into a tier. Tiers reflect current skill and commitment, not seniority, and are reviewed regularly.

Tier 3 · General Memberabout 80 places
Interested students, including beginners. Seminars, salons, company visits, introductory workshops and open club activities. No external competition representation.
Tier 2 · Training Memberabout 30 places
Members with foundational knowledge and reliable participation. Structured bi-weekly training, supervised hands-on work, internal challenges and support to project teams.
Tier 1 · Advanced Memberabout 15 places
Technically strong and highly committed members. Form and lead project and competition teams, write proposals, mentor junior members and represent PolyU when selected.

Commitment and club rules

  1. Keep your commitment. At the interview you agree how much time you will give to the club. We expect you to keep it. Members who repeatedly fall short will be advised to leave the club.
  2. Follow the club rules, always. Every member completes safety training and signs the club's safety, equipment and membership rules before working in the Industrial Centre. A first violation brings a written warning and retraining; a second violation means removal from the club. A single serious breach can lead to immediate removal.
